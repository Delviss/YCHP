Vision & Roadmap

Yana, by Your Curated Holiday Planner. Condensed from "Yana – Product Vision & Roadmap", v1.0, September 2026. Confidential: for product, design, development and partnership planning.

Curated travel. A smarter way to save for it.

This is the plain-language reference for what Yana is building, why it matters, how the experience should feel and the path to launch. It stays free of technical detail. For that, see the companion Product Requirements Document (every feature, in full) and Technical Requirements Document (the build approach for web and mobile).

What changed from the earlier YCHP vision

  • The product is now called Yana, presented as Yana by Your Curated Holiday Planner.
  • Group Saving moves into Phase 1. It is treated as a launch feature unless a technical or legal dependency makes that impossible.
  • Cruises and gift cards are confirmed for Phase 3, alongside car hire and buy now, choose dates later.
  • The interface direction moves to a light, blue-led palette (see the Brand Guidelines).
  • New launch priority rule: if something has to move to protect timing, Hotels, Deals, Experiences, Curation and both wallet modes are protected first.

1. The vision

Yana is a curated travel platform designed to make exceptional holidays feel achievable, not intimidating. It combines selected luxury stays and experiences with a built-in wallet that lets people save toward travel over time, either on their own or together with family and friends.

The Yana promise: Turn "one day" into "we're going", by helping people discover, save for and book exceptional travel.

Pillar What it means
Curated, not cluttered A considered set of stays, offers and experiences rather than endless search results.
Save your way Put money aside for a trip over time instead of treating travel as one large, all-at-once expense.
Travel together Group saving makes shared holidays easier to organise, fund and understand.
Premium, still welcoming Luxury in the experience and design, without making the product feel exclusive or intimidating.

What launches at the centre of Yana

2. Why Yana exists

Travel aspiration is high. The journey to booking is still harder than it should be.

2.1 The problem

Many travellers can afford to travel internationally over time, but the current booking experience assumes confidence, familiarity and the ability to pay a large amount at once. Mainstream platforms often add more choice when what people need is more confidence.

  • Too much choice can make travel research feel like work rather than anticipation.
  • A single large payment can make a premium holiday feel further away than it really is.
  • Group trips are often coordinated through chats, spreadsheets and informal transfers, with little visibility over who has contributed what.
  • First-time or less-frequent international travellers may need reassurance, not just inventory.

2.2 The opportunity

Yana brings discovery, saving and booking into one calm experience. It is built for African travellers first, while the destinations and product ambition are global. The brand should feel contemporary and universal: not themed around a single country, language or idea of what "Africa" looks like.

A product principle: Yana is not a budget travel app. It is a luxury travel platform that gives people a more realistic way to fund and organise exceptional trips.

2.3 Who Yana serves

Audience What they need How Yana responds
Travellers Beautiful options they can trust, with a manageable path to booking Curated stays and deals, clear pricing, a wallet, group saving and guidance
Property partners Incremental bookings without losing control of rates and availability A partner profile, inventory and availability tools, payouts, promotions and reporting
Yana team Control of quality, money, support and merchandising Admin controls for approvals, bookings, payments, promotions, disputes and reporting

3. The product experience

From inspiration to departure, Yana should feel like one journey. The next step should be obvious at every stage. Discovery creates desire. The wallet turns desire into progress. Booking turns progress into a confirmed trip.

Step What it covers
01 Discover Hotels, Deals, Experiences
02 Save Personal or group wallet
03 Book Clear pricing and checkout
04 Go Trip details, support and rebooking

3.1 The five launch destinations inside the app

  • Hotels. Search and browse a considered range of stays with strong photography, transparent pricing, availability, policies, maps and relevant filters.
  • Deals. Yana-curated offers that combine price, inclusions and timing into something genuinely worth noticing, not a wall of discounts.
  • Curation. A short request form for travellers who want YCHP-style end-to-end help rather than a self-serve booking journey.
  • Experiences. Dining, spa, tours, activities and other add-ons that can turn a room booking into a full trip.
  • Wallet. The financial planning layer of Yana: save personally, contribute as a group, see progress and move from intention to booking.

3.2 Merchandising without clutter

Destination-based and retargeted promotions should feel editorial and relevant. Advertising can exist inside Yana, but it should never make the interface feel like a discount marketplace.

4. The Yana Wallet

The wallet is not an add-on. It is one of the reasons Yana exists.

The wallet changes the psychology of travel from "Can I pay for this today?" to "How do I get there by the time I want to go?" It should make progress visible, group saving transparent and the eventual booking feel earned rather than impulsive.

Mode What it does
Personal Wallet Create a travel goal, add money over time, see the balance and understand how close the trip is.
Group Saving Friends or family save toward one trip, with clear contribution targets and visibility over each person's progress.
Trip-linked goals Savings can be connected to a destination, hotel, deal or target amount rather than sitting as an abstract balance.

4.1 Group saving experience

The group flow borrows the transparency of cost-splitting tools such as Tricount while remaining clearly Yana. Each member should understand the total trip goal, their share, what they have contributed and what remains.

  • Create or join a group trip.
  • Set the trip target and contribution split.
  • See individual and group progress in real time.
  • Send reminders and contribution notifications without exposing unnecessary financial information.
  • Move from funded goal to booking without repeating the planning process.

4.2 Trust and regulation

Because the wallet may hold customer funds, it is built with a licensed financial partner and reviewed for each launch market. The current direction is to work with First National Bank (FNB) on the wallet and savings framework, while Paystack remains part of the payment infrastructure for bookings and commission flows.

Design rule: The wallet should feel motivating, not like internet banking. Progress, destination imagery and trip milestones should carry as much visual weight as balances.

5. Marketplace, supply and commercial model

Global places. A product shaped around how Yana customers actually travel.

5.1 Supply strategy

Yana launches with a globally distributed property portfolio, with strong concentration across Asia and the Middle East and an expanding set of properties across Africa. These regions combine strong travel appeal, competitive accommodation pricing and major air hubs, while giving the platform room to merchandise both aspiration and value.

5.2 Launch demand markets

Paid customer acquisition initially focuses on Zimbabwe, South Africa, Zambia, Kenya, Nigeria and Ghana, while the platform remains open to organic demand from elsewhere. Prices display in the traveller's local currency, with USD and ZAR required from launch, and English and French supported from day one.

5.3 How Yana earns

The commercial direction is a commission of roughly 15–20% on bookings. For prepaid transactions, the preferred approach is an automatic payment split so the property receives its share and Yana's commission is separated at the point of payment. Pay-at-property bookings need a separate commission collection process.

5.4 Property partner experience

Partner need Yana capability
Keep inventory accurate Connect through a channel manager rather than building one-off integrations to each property system.
Control availability and pricing Property dashboard for rooms, pricing, blocked dates, discounts and promotions.
Know what is happening Bookings, guest messages, reviews, revenue, payouts and reporting in one place.
Protect brand quality Yana verification and admin approval before properties or offers go live.

6. Roadmap

Launch the product people will remember Yana for, then widen the ecosystem.

The roadmap protects the launch date without cutting the features that create Yana's identity. In particular, curation and the wallet are not "nice to have" features.

Phase Product focus What is included
Phase 1 · Launch Yana core Accounts; Hotels; Deals; Curation; Experiences; personal Wallet; Group Saving; property pages; search and filters; booking; payments; cancellations and refunds; reviews; messaging; notifications; partner dashboard; verification; admin and promotions.
Phase 2 · Expand Planning + discovery AI travel assistant with user memory and preferences; flights shown in a Skyscanner-style comparison flow and linked out to the airline or agent; deeper activity inventory; smarter destination recommendations.
Phase 3 · Grow Broader travel ecosystem Car hire; cruises; gift cards; more homes and apartments; more markets, currencies and languages; buy now, choose dates later once partner and legal mechanics are confirmed.

Why the phases are sequenced this way. Phase 1 establishes the product people will associate with Yana: curated travel plus a practical way to fund it. Phase 2 deepens planning and discovery once that core journey is stable. Phase 3 broadens the ecosystem only after the commercial and partner mechanics are proven.

Launch priority: If a feature must move to protect timing, protect Hotels, Deals, Experiences, Curation and both wallet modes first. Those are the product identity.

7. Delivery plan

An indicative shape, measured in weeks from the day the build starts.

7.1 Launch dependencies

Dependency What needs to happen
Wallet + legal Confirm the licensed partner structure, custody of funds and market-by-market compliance early.
Supply + availability Channel-manager integration and property onboarding must run in parallel with the build.
Trial group Use a small invited cohort to test discovery, saving, group contributions and booking before public launch.

Timing principle: The product should launch when the wallet, group saving and booking journey are trustworthy. A rushed date is less valuable than a complete Yana experience.

8. Brand and interface direction

Yana should feel fresh, optimistic and premium, never grandstanding. The interface carries the calm of a luxury travel brand but must stay accessible enough to use every week while saving. Blue is the signature: bright enough to feel like travel and possibility, grounded by a deeper ocean tone for trust and financial moments.

The full palette, developer colour tokens, contrast guidance and UI principles live in the Brand Guidelines.

9. Success and decisions

9.1 What success looks like

  • A traveller can go from inspiration to a confirmed booking without feeling lost or overwhelmed.
  • People return to Yana because their wallet and group trips give them a reason to keep planning between bookings.
  • Group contributions are clear enough that families and friends no longer need separate spreadsheets or informal tracking.
  • Property partners see real bookings and can manage availability, promotions and payouts without heavy support.
  • The platform feels calm and premium, but not distant, formal or intimidating.
  • Yana can grow beyond the first launch markets without changing its core identity.

9.2 Decisions that still need to stay visible

Decision Current direction Why it matters
Wallet structure + regulation Build with a licensed financial partner; current direction is FNB Determines custody of funds, market-by-market compliance and launch readiness.
Payment routing Paystack with automatic commission split for prepaid bookings Keeps commission and refunds cleaner and more scalable.
Group Saving at launch Treat as Phase 1 unless a technical or legal dependency makes this impossible It is a defining differentiator, not a peripheral feature.
Supply connectivity Use a channel manager Keeps pricing and availability accurate across a large property inventory.
Buy now, choose dates later Hold for Phase 3 while partner mechanics are finalised Requires clear inventory, expiry and refund rules before release.

Earlier decisions that still stand: a single app that adapts to whether someone is a traveller or a property partner (rather than two apps), and the launch market, currency and language choices in Section 5.

9.3 Next working session

Confirm the Phase 1 scope, the wallet partner and legal path, the launch catalogue plan and the final design system. From there, design moves directly into the traveller, group-saving, partner and admin journeys.

The north star: If Yana works, people should stop saying "maybe one day" and start saying "we are already saving for it."
